## LinkedIn company page post

68.6% of Denver's active long-term rental licenses are their holder's only license.

The register pulled October 20, 2026 holds 38,970 rows - but a row is not a license. Rows sharing a license number are the unit filings of one license: the largest license carries 697 rows, one per unit address of a single apartment property, while other licenses cover a whole building in one row. Counted by license number, Denver has 26,941 active long-term rental licenses under 20,780 holders. 18,471 holders hold exactly one license; the largest holder holds 525 (1.95%), and the 65 holders with 11 or more licenses hold 8.4% between them. 37.1% of licenses sit under a business-entity name, against 0% in the companion short-term rental brief (October 19, 2026).

The footprint is citywide: active rows appear in all 78 neighborhoods, at 108.4 rows per 1,000 housing units citywide and 286.1 in Whittier, the same neighborhood that tops the short-term rate table. The brief also prints what the register cannot say: licensed parcels carry 30.1% of the city's assessor-record units, and no licensed share of rental stock can be computed from the published data, because no renter-occupied denominator exists in it.
